================================================================================
NEURIPS REVIEWER #3 — METHODOLOGY & BENCHMARK DESIGN
================================================================================

Reviewer Profile: NeurIPS reviewer with background in evaluation
methodology, benchmark design, and low-resource NLP. Published at
NeurIPS, EMNLP, and COLING. Reviewed for MMLU, HellaSwag, and BIG-bench.

---

STRENGTHS
----------

1. Cross-lingual coverage (English/Arabic/Yoruba) is genuinely valuable.
   Most proverb benchmarks are monolingual or Indo-European only.

2. The two-strategy design (S1 baseline + S2 adversarial) is sound.
   This follows established practice in benchmark design (e.g., MMLU-Pro's
   easy vs. hard splits).

3. Position bias mitigation via seeded shuffling is methodologically
   correct and well-implemented.

4. The contamination filter shows awareness of data leakage issues.

5. Pre-flight health checks prevent silent model failures from corrupting
   results. This is a genuine methodological contribution that other
   benchmarks could adopt.

6. The API infrastructure hardening (retry, cooldown, dynamic workers)
   documents real-world challenges in LLM-based evaluation. The negative
   results (v4.3.3 collapse) are instructive.

---

WEAKNESSES
-----------

1. NO HUMAN BASELINE. A benchmark without human performance is
   uninterpretable. If humans score 95% on S1 and 90% on S2, then model
   scores of 87%/63% are poor. If humans score 70%/40%, models are doing
   well. Without this calibration, the numbers are meaningless.

2. NOVELTY IS MARGINAL. ProverbEval (NAACL 2025) already covers
   Ethiopian languages with MCQ, FiB, and generation tasks. Jawaher
   (NAACL 2025) has 10,037 Arabic proverbs. MasalBench (2026) covers
   Persian. The contribution of adding English/Arabic/Yoruba is
   incremental, not transformative.

3. TASK IS TOO NARROW. Only multiple-choice discrimination is tested.
   NeurIPS D&B track favors benchmarks that test multiple capabilities.
   ProverbEval's three-task design (MCQ + FiB + generation) is stronger.

4. COMMITTEE IS TOO SMALL. Two models (one of which is Arabic-centric)
   is not a robust committee. A 2-model committee cannot detect
   idiosyncratic failures. Standard practice is 3-5 models.

5. FALLBACK RATE IS CONCERNING. Even at 0% in the local test, the
   historical 21.3% fallback means ~1 in 5 items had placeholder
   distractors. These items are trivial and inflate accuracy. The
   paper must report accuracy EXCLUDING fallback items.

6. NO EMBEDDING-BASED DIVERSITY SCORING. The S2 distractors are
   generated by a single model (qwen3-32b). Without diversity metrics,
   the distractors may be homogeneous. ProverbEval and MMLU-Pro report
   inter-distractor similarity.

---

COMPARISON TO EXISTING BENCHMARKS
----------------------------------

| Benchmark | Languages | Tasks | Items | Human Val | NeurIPS Fit |
|-----------|-----------|-------|-------|-----------|-------------|
| ProverbEval | Ethiopian (5) | MCQ+FiB+Gen | ~2,500 | Yes | Strong D&B |
| Jawaher | Arabic (20 dialects) | Trans+Expl | 10,037 | Yes | Strong D&B |
| MasalBench | Persian | MCQ | ~1,000 | Yes | Moderate D&B |
| PRONE | Nepali | MCQ | 2,830 | Yes | Moderate D&B |
| ProverbGap | EN+AR+YO | MCQ only | 2,250 | NO | Weak D&B |

ProverbGap is competitive in language coverage but lags in:
- Number of tasks (1 vs. 3)
- Human validation (0% vs. 100% of test set)
- Total items (2,250 vs. 2,500-10,000)

---

ACCEPTANCE PROBABILITY
-----------------------

With current state (N=750/language, no human validation, 1 task,
2-model committee, unresolved data provenance):

NeurIPS Main:        ~3%  (would not reach full review)
NeurIPS D&B:         ~15% (would likely be rejected in full review)

With improvements (N=1,000/language, human validation, FiB + generation
tasks, 4-model committee, resolved provenance):

NeurIPS D&B:         ~25-30% (competitive but not guaranteed)

---

BOTTOM LINE
------------

The methodology is competent but not groundbreaking. The pipeline
hardening (health checks, retries, position bias mitigation) is the
strongest methodological contribution. However, NeurIPS D&B reviewers
will compare this to ProverbEval and MasalBench, which are more
comprehensive. To be competitive, ProverbGap needs to match or exceed
them on tasks, validation, and scale.
