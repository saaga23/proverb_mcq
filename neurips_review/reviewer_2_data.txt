================================================================================
NEURIPS REVIEWER #2 — DATA QUALITY & PROVENANCE
================================================================================

Reviewer Profile: NeurIPS D&B track reviewer, focus on dataset ethics,
licensing, and documentation. Published data papers at NeurIPS, ACL,
and FAccT. Strong stance on data provenance.

---

CRITICAL CONCERN #1: YORUBA DATA FROM A BOOK
----------------------------------------------

The Yoruba proverbs come from "a book" (user statement). This raises
immediate questions:

- Which book? Title, author, publisher, year?
- Was permission obtained from the author/publisher?
- Is the book in the public domain or under copyright?
- If under copyright, does fair use apply to extracting proverbs for a
  benchmark? (Proverbs are traditional knowledge, but a curated collection
  may have copyright in the selection and arrangement.)
- Are the translations and cultural contexts ALSO from the book, or were
  they added by the authors?

NeurIPS requires datasets to have clear licenses. "From a book" is not
a license. Without explicit permission or public-domain status, this is
a RED FLAG for any venue, especially NeurIPS which has tightened data
ethics requirements.

RECOMMENDATION: Obtain explicit written permission from the book's
publisher, OR verify the book is public domain, OR replace with
openly licensed Yoruba proverb collections.

---

CRITICAL CONCERN #2: ENGLISH DATA FROM WEB SCRAPING
----------------------------------------------------

Web scraping for dataset construction is increasingly scrutinized:

- Which websites were scraped?
- What was the scraping protocol (rate, headers, robots.txt compliance)?
- Was there deduplication against known LLM training data?
- Are the scraped sources themselves original or also aggregated?
- What is the license of the scraped content?

The paper mentions a "contamination filter" but does not describe the
scraping methodology. NeurIPS reviewers will ask for a DATASET CARD
with full provenance documentation.

The risk: If the English proverbs were scraped from sites like
Wiktionary, QuoteGarden, or similar, those sites may have terms of
service that prohibit bulk extraction. This is a legal and ethical risk.

RECOMMENDATION: Document the full scraping protocol. If sources have
restrictive ToS, obtain permission or replace with openly licensed data.

---

CRITICAL CONCERN #3: ARABIC DATA FROM HUGGINGFACE
--------------------------------------------------

The Arabic data was "from HuggingFace." This is better than scraping
BUT:

- Which HuggingFace dataset? (There are multiple Arabic proverb datasets.)
- What is the license of the original dataset?
- Was the dataset modified? If so, under what terms?
- Does the original dataset have a paper or documentation?
- Was Cultural_Context added by the authors or present in the original?

Using HuggingFace data without proper attribution is a citation ethics
violation. NeurIPS reviewers check for this.

RECOMMENDATION: Cite the exact HuggingFace dataset, its license, and
document all modifications made.

---

CRITICAL CONCERN #4: NO HUMAN VALIDATION
-----------------------------------------

A benchmark without human validation is not credible. For N=750 per
language, at minimum:
- 50 items (7%) should be validated by native speakers for each language
- Validation should check: unambiguity, answerability, option uniqueness,
  cultural accuracy

The paper currently has ZERO human validation. This is unacceptable for
NeurIPS D&B track.

---

OVERALL DATA VERDICT
---------------------

The data provenance is INSUFFICIENT for NeurIPS. Three of three data
sources have unresolved legal/ethical questions. The lack of human
validation compounds the problem.

This is a MAJOR REVISION issue, not a minor fix. Before any submission:
1. Resolve all licensing questions
2. Add human validation
3. Publish a comprehensive dataset card

Without these, acceptance probability is near zero regardless of
track or methodological quality.
