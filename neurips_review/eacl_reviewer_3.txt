================================================================================
EACL 2027 REVIEWER #3 — METHODOLOGY & STATISTICAL RIGOR
================================================================================

Reviewer Profile: EACL reviewer with statistics background, focus on
experimental design, significance testing, and benchmark validity.
Published at ACL, EMNLP, and NeurIPS. Reviewed for MMLU-Pro and
ProverbEval.

---

METHODOLOGICAL STRENGTHS
-------------------------

1. **Two-strategy design is correctly motivated.**
   S1 (negative sampling) establishes a baseline. S2 (cross-family LLM
   paraphrasing) creates adversarial distractors. The McNemar test
   confirms a significant difficulty gap. This follows best practice
   in benchmark design (e.g., MMLU-Pro's easy vs. hard splits).

2. **Cross-family generator choice is defensible.**
   Using qwen3-32b (Alibaba) to generate distractors evaluated by
   llama-4 (Meta) and allam-2-7b (Saudi) avoids same-family
   exploitation. The paper documents this rationale well.

3. **Position bias mitigation is rigorous.**
   Seeded shuffling per model is correct. The chi-square position bias
   test is the right statistical tool.

4. **Contamination filter is a good defensive practice.**
   Excluding known LLM training data prevents inflated accuracy.

5. **The v4.3.3→v4.3.4→v4.3.6 evolution narrative is valuable.**
   The API infrastructure collapse (769/790 failures), the degenerate
   unanimity artifact, and the Yoruba think-tag parser bug are genuine
   negative results. The methodology section documenting these is one
   of the paper's strongest contributions.

---

METHODOLOGICAL WEAKNESSES
--------------------------

1. **NO HUMAN BASELINE.**
   This cannot be overstated. Without human performance, model scores
   are uninterpretable. If humans score 95%/90% on S1/S2, then the
   models are failing. If humans score 70%/40%, the models are doing
   well. The paper currently assumes 100% human performance without
   evidence.

2. **McNemar assumptions are questionable.**
   The paper reports McNemar p<0.001 comparing S1 vs S2. But:
   - Are the items the same across strategies? (Yes, same proverbs)
   - Are the models the same? (Yes, same committee)
   - BUT: S2 has fallback items (~6.7% in local test, historically 21.3%).
     These items are excluded from S2-strict accuracy but included in
     S1. The denominators differ. McNemar requires the same N.

   **Fix:** Run McNemar on NON-FALLBACK items only, or report two
   separate tests.

3. **Bootstrap CI without stratification.**
   The 95% CI [58.1%, 66.6%] mixes fallback items (~100% accuracy)
   with genuine S2 items (~50% accuracy). The CI is not interpretable.

   **Fix:** Report stratified CIs: (a) all items, (b) non-fallback only,
   (c) fallback only.

4. **N=150 pilot is underpowered for cross-lingual claims.**
   With 50 items per language, the standard error is ~7%. The observed
   gap between English (71.3%) and Yoruba (46.2%) has overlapping CIs.
   The claim that "English > Yoruba" may not survive significance
   testing with Bonferroni correction.

   **Fix:** Scale to N=750 per language (already planned). The point
   estimates will be more reliable.

5. **The 2-model committee is too small.**
   With only allam-2-7b active (llama-4 dropped due to API failures),
   "unanimous consensus" means one model agreeing with itself. This is
   a degenerate case, not a strong signal.

   **Fix:** Add 2 more models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, or a local
   model like Llama-3.3-70b). Report consensus metrics only with
   committees of ≥3 models.

6. **No embedding-based diversity scoring.**
   S2 distractors are generated by a single model. Without diversity
   metrics, they may be semantically homogeneous. Standard practice
   (MMLU-Pro, ProverbEval) reports inter-distractor embedding distance.

   **Fix:** Add sentence-transformer-based diversity scoring. Report
   average cosine distance between distractors.

COMPARISON TO EXISTING BENCHMARKS
----------------------------------

| Feature | ProverbGap (current) | ProverbEval | MMLU-Pro | Gap |
|---------|----------------------|-------------|----------|-----|
| Languages | 3 | 5 | 1 (EN) | Comparable |
| Tasks | 1 (MCQ) | 3 | 1 (MCQ) | Inferior |
| Items | 2,250 | ~2,500 | ~3,000 | Comparable |
| Human validation | 0% | 100% | 100% | **Inferior** |

| Generation task | No | Yes | No | Inferior |
| Cost reporting | "~$50" | Exact | Exact | Inferior |

ProverbGap is competitive on language coverage but lags on validation,
committee size, and task diversity.

---

THE NEGATIVE RESULTS ARE VALUABLE
----------------------------------

The paper's strongest unique contribution is the documentation of
real-world LLM evaluation failures:
- API infrastructure fragility (v4.3.3 collapse)
- Same-family model exploitation
- CoT reasoning collapse (llama-4: 23/24 NaN)
- Think-tag parser bugs (qwen3-32b unclosed `<think>`)
- Rate-limit cascade dynamics

These are genuine findings that the community needs. No other benchmark
paper documents API failures with this level of detail.

**Recommendation:** Expand the negative results section. This could be
a standalone contribution that elevates the paper above "just another
benchmark."

ACCEPTANCE PROBABILITY
-----------------------

Current state: **~20%**

With human baseline, expanded committee, diversity metrics, and

---

BOTTOM LINE
------------

The methodology is competent but not exceptional. The statistical
analysis needs restructuring. The negative results are the hidden gem.
If the authors lean into the "lessons from building a robust LLM
benchmark" narrative, this paper becomes much more interesting than
a standard benchmark paper.
