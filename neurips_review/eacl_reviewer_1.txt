================================================================================
EACL 2027 REVIEWER #1 — MULTILINGUAL & LOW-RESOURCE NLP
================================================================================

Reviewer Profile: EACL senior AC, specialization in African NLP,
Arabic NLP, and cross-lingual evaluation. Published at EACL, ACL,
NAACL, and LREC. Native Arabic speaker, speaks Yoruba.

---

STRENGTHS
----------

1. **Yoruba coverage is genuinely novel.** EACL audiences care deeply about
   low-resource African languages. There are very few benchmarks for Yoruba,
   and even fewer for figurative language. This fills a real gap.

2. **Arabic with Cultural_Context is richer than typical translation tasks.**
   Most Arabic benchmarks test MSA or dialectal classification. A proverb
   meaning comprehension task with cultural explanations is novel and
   culturally situated.

3. **The meaning-comprehension reframe is methodologically sound.**
   The reviewer read the v4.3.4→v4.3.6 evolution. Moving from translation
   matching to meaning comprehension corrected a fundamental design flaw.
   The 36%→0% Yoruba fallback improvement is strong evidence the reframe
   worked.

4. **The two-strategy design (baseline + adversarial) follows best practice.**
   S1 establishes a floor, S2 tests adversarial robustness. The 24pp gap
   (86.8%→62.5%) is a meaningful difficulty gradient.

5. **Position bias mitigation is correctly implemented.**
   Seeded shuffling per model is the right approach.

6. **API infrastructure hardening is a genuine contribution.**
   The v4.3.3 collapse narrative (769/790 API failures, single-model
   degenerate unanimity) and the defensive mitigations (health checks,
   retries, dynamic workers) are instructive for the community.

---

WEAKNESSES
-----------

1. **NO HUMAN VALIDATION.** This is the single biggest weakness. For a
   benchmark claiming to test "cultural meaning comprehension," there is
   zero evidence that native speakers of Arabic or Yoruba find the items
   valid. A single misaligned item undermines the entire benchmark.

   EACL reviewers are particularly sensitive to this because:
   - We have seen too many "African language" papers written by authors
     with no connection to the language community
   - Cultural accuracy cannot be verified by non-native speakers
   - The QA_Flag system (FIX/DROP/PERFECT) suggests the Yoruba data was
     curated by someone, but we don't know who or by what criteria

2. **Data provenance is unresolved.**
   - Yoruba: "from a book" — which book? Copyright status?
   - English: "web scraping" — which sites? Compliance with robots.txt?
   - Arabic: "from HuggingFace" — which dataset? License?

   EACL has adopted the ACL data provenance policy. Papers without clear
   licensing documentation are increasingly desk-rejected.

3. **Only 1 task (MCQ).**
   EACL audiences are familiar with ProverbEval (NAACL 2025), which has
   MCQ + FiB + Generation. A single-task benchmark feels thin by
   comparison. The generation task is especially important for proverb
   understanding — MCQ only tests discrimination, not production.

4. **Committee is effectively 1 model.**
   The paper reports a 2-model committee (llama-4-maverick + allam-2-7b)
   but notes llama-4 was dropped due to API failures. The actual
   evaluation uses only allam-2-7b. A single-model evaluation is not a
   committee — it's a single point of failure.

5. **N=750 per language is sufficient but not impressive.**
   For EACL's Resources & Evaluation track, 2,250 total items is
   respectable but not extraordinary. Jawaher has 10,037 Arabic proverbs.
   ProverbEval has ~2,500 items across 5 languages with 3 tasks each.

---

EACL-SPECIFIC CONCERNS
-----------------------

1. **The "European" angle is weak.** EACL is the European chapter, but
   the paper has no European language (French/German/Spanish are mentioned
   as future work). Why is this at EACL and not ACL or NAACL?

   Suggested fix: Add French/German/Spanish to the pilot. The actual_data
   already has these files. Even N=100 per language would strengthen the
   European connection.

2. **No connection to African NLP community.**
   The Yoruba data is valuable, but the paper has no African co-authors,
   no acknowledgement of Yoruba cultural consultants, and no citation of
   African NLP work (e.g., Masakhane, Lacuna Fund). EACL reviewers will
   notice this.

---

ACCEPTANCE PROBABILITY FOR EACL 2027
-------------------------------------

Current state (N=750, no human validation, 1 task, 1 model, unresolved
provenance):

**Acceptance probability: ~20-25%**

With improvements (human validation, FiB task, 4-model committee,
resolved provenance, European languages added):

**Acceptance probability: ~35-40%**

EACL is slightly less competitive than ACL (~21% vs ~25%), and the
multilingual/low-resource angle is genuinely valued. But the current
gaps are significant.

---

BOTTOM LINE
------------

This is a **BORDERLINE PAPER** for EACL 2027. The data and methodology
are competent, but the lack of human validation and unresolved data
provenance are serious weaknesses. With 10 weeks until the August 3
deadline, these issues are fixable. But they MUST be fixed.
